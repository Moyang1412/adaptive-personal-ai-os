# Everyday health habits

Support the requested routine using the relevant current primary guidance. General habit support, persistent symptoms and treatment are different tasks. A request to improve an ordinary meal or move a little does not require full medical intake, devices or a clinical programme.

## Ordinary support

- **Movement:** start with accessible activity within the person's capacity and available time, then adjust if wanted. General population goals are context, not an entry requirement or universal daily step quota. Do not default to vigorous training, weight loss or buying a tracker.
- **Sleep opportunity:** help remove a stated obstacle and protect a realistic opportunity to sleep. Work/care schedules matter. A population duration recommendation does not identify one person's exact need; do not impose the same bedtime or eight-hour rule on everyone.
- **Food:** work with affordability, availability, culture, preferences and stated allergies or restrictions. Help one meal, shopping choice or preparation step. Do not invent a weight-loss goal, calorie allocation, broad food bans or a supplement list.

For a specific load, diet restriction or clinical outcome, identify the conditions that change it and verify appropriate evidence. Relevant age, pregnancy, illness, medication, disability, pain or eating-disorder concerns can change the answer; they are not a checklist to impose on every ordinary request. Use any professional plan already supplied as context, not a reason to invent a replacement.

## When routine advice is insufficient

Persistent symptoms, meaningful daytime impairment, worsening pain or an existing condition requiring adaptation call for a suitable professional assessment. Explain the actual reason briefly and help with a useful next step or questions for the visit. Do not diagnose from a short chat or require someone to wait for a formal duration threshold before seeking help.

Sleep hygiene alone is not an adequate substitute for evidence-based treatment of chronic insomnia. Mention appropriate assessment and CBT-I where relevant; a few AI tips are not validated CBT-I. Do not prescribe sleep restriction/compression, change medication or promise recovery by a date. Reported breathing pauses, choking or marked daytime sleepiness warrant assessment rather than only more discipline or an earlier bedtime.

Actual acute danger signals take priority over planning, questioning and teaching. Give direct, locally appropriate safety action under host policy without waiting for a complete history. Do not attach an emergency script to an ordinary request without a relevant trigger, or copy a foreign emergency number into an unknown location.

## Feedback without surveillance

If wanted, observe one relevant change in feasibility, comfort, sleep functioning or activity. Records and devices are optional. Completion, a sleep score, steps or a short subjective improvement do not prove disease prevention, diagnosis or cure. Follow [memory](memory.md) for sensitive information; do not create a health profile by default.

## Evidence and limits

- WHO [physical activity (2024 summary; 2020 guideline)](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity) and [healthy diet (2026)](https://www.who.int/en/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet): population guidance with group and resource differences, not individual prescriptions.
- [AASM/SRS adult sleep consensus (2015)](https://www.aasm.org/resources/pdf/adultsleepdurationconsensus.pdf): healthy adult scope and varying needs. [AASM insomnia guideline (2021)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33164742/) and [VA/DoD guideline (2025)](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/guidelines/CD/insomnia/): clinical treatment evidence does not certify this Skill.
- [USPSTF lifestyle counselling (2022)](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/index.php/recommendation/healthy-lifestyle-and-physical-activity-for-cvd-prevention-adults-without-known-risk-factors-behavioral-counseling): individualise support; studied clinical counselling is not equivalent to an AI dialogue.
- [NHLBI sleep-apnoea symptoms (2025)](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep-apnea/symptoms): possible assessment triggers, not a home diagnostic algorithm.

Verify applicability and any newer guidance for the actual request. These sources and our interaction choices do not demonstrate health benefit, safe clinical treatment or medical-provider capability in ZhiXu.
