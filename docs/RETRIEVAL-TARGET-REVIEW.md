# Retrieval targets and transfer · 2026-10-17

## Evidence inspected

[Pan & Rickard (2017), author-hosted paper](https://sc-pan.github.io/pdf/PR_JEPA_2017.pdf), DOI 10.1037/xap0000124: inspected abstract, experimental design/procedure and discussion/practical limits. Four experiments trained term-definition facts with multiple-choice questions and feedback; final tests followed 48 hours later. Benefits and transfer depended on whether the comparator was no training or restudy. Against restudy, transfer to the reversed response was at best small. This does not establish that retrieval never transfers: the authors explicitly distinguish other transfer contexts and more elaborate practice. We adopt the narrow caution that identifying a term does not establish the ability to explain it. No paper text, materials or learner data were copied.

[Butowska-Buczyńska et al. (2024), PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39453748/), DOI 10.1073/pnas.2413511121: abstract inspected; publisher request timed out and full PMC page returned a browser challenge. Six vocabulary experiments and a seventh lecture experiment report benefits of variable retrieval cues, while learners preferred constant cues. Full methods were not verified here. This is a reason not to equate easier-feeling repetition with better learning, not a validated schedule or a claim that arbitrary prompt variation helps every domain. No access challenge was bypassed.

## Before and after

The existing protocol already separated retention from transfer and required fresh independent tasks. It did not explicitly guide the choice of response direction or distinguish recognition from production when choosing practice. A short learning-reference section now makes these distinctions without introducing a mandatory diagnostic or second flashcard database. Four authored scenarios test the specification boundaries: reverse-direction overclaim, target-aligned practice, unambiguous direct translation, and ambiguous changed wording. These are instructions and schema cases, not observed model or learner outcomes.
